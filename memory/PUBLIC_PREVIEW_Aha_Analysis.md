# Public Preview — The One "Aha"
## Choosing the single organizing realization

> Analysis only. No implementation. The question: what is the ONE memorable realization the preview must manufacture — the destination every element (seed, branching, sequence, ending) is judged against?

## Framing the decision
Teachers already believe AI can *produce* essays — that is precisely the threat they associate with tools like ChatGPT. So a preview that merely shows Compass writing well, or even "diagnosing," competes on a spectrum they already distrust. The winning realization must move Compass into a *different category* in the teacher's mind — from "text generator" to "something that teaches." The aha should be the felt difference between **being given an answer** and **being brought to understand**.

## Candidate ahas

### A. "It won't do the work for me — it makes *me* the writer." (authorship / anti-coauthoring)
- **Strengths:** sharp, immediate contrast with ChatGPT; strikes the exact nerve teachers care about (student cheating). Easy to trigger (teacher asks it to write; it declines in character).
- **Weaknesses:** high risk of reading as a *limitation* — "it refused to help." Without a felt payoff, a time-poor teacher concludes "less useful," not "fundamentally different." Refusal alone is negative space; it needs something to be *for*.

### B. "It read *me*, not my sentence — it diagnosed my thinking." (diagnosis over grading)
- **Strengths:** lands fast (turn 1); genuinely uncanny; it's the move no autocomplete makes and the move great teachers make. Builds instant credibility.
- **Weaknesses:** impressive but *observational* — the teacher admires Compass rather than experiencing their own change. Depends on Compass's read being visibly accurate every time. It's a great hook, but as a *terminal* realization it keeps the teacher a spectator of Compass's cleverness.

### C. "It didn't give me an answer — it led me until I stated the understanding myself." (self-constructed insight through scaffolding)
- **Strengths:** this is the *felt* experience of learning, which is the category difference. It is self-evidencing and durable: the teacher literally hears their own words become the lesson — you can't argue with an insight you produced. It **subsumes the other candidates**: to bring someone to self-articulation, Compass must diagnose (B), withhold the answer and not coauthor (A), and withhold convention until the need is felt (D). It also generates E as the downstream conclusion.
- **Weaknesses:** requires the full ~4-minute arc; riskiest to land if the conversation stalls; needs a reliable path to the transfer beat.

### D. "It taught me the concept only *after* I needed it — no jargon dump." (meaning before convention)
- **Strengths:** elegant and distinctive; explains the calm, uncluttered feel.
- **Weaknesses:** mostly *absence* (of jargon) — teachers rarely consciously register what didn't happen. It's a supporting texture, not a headline. Weakest as a dominant aha.

### E. "This is what I wish I could give every student — and it just did it to me." (scalable expert tutor)
- **Strengths:** strongest emotional/commercial pull; maps directly to the teacher's job pain (150 students, no time).
- **Weaknesses:** it's a *conclusion drawn from* the other ahas, not an in-experience moment. You cannot engineer it directly; attempting to makes the preview feel like a pitch. Best treated as the intended after-effect, not the organizing principle.

## Recommendation: **C — "It didn't give me an answer; it led me until I said the understanding myself."**

C is the correct organizing principle because it is the only candidate that is (1) *experienced* rather than observed, (2) *self-evidencing* (the proof is the teacher's own sentence), and (3) *inclusive* — it forces every other strength into service rather than competing with them. A and D become the *mechanism* of C; B becomes the *on-ramp* to C; E becomes the *consequence* of C.

Reframed as a one-line design north star:
> **"I came in expecting it to write for me. I left having taught myself something — and it never wrote a word."**

## Evaluating each preview element against Aha C

| Element | Contribution to C | Verdict / refinement |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening seed** ("one belief, plain words") | Guarantees the eventual principle is grounded in the teacher's *own* meaning, so the closing insight is unmistakably theirs, not Compass's. | **Keep, essential.** Tighten so the belief is one the teacher has real stake in — ownership of the seed becomes ownership of the aha. |
| **Diagnosis / branching (A/B/C + strong-writer restraint)** | Earns the credibility (candidate B) that makes the teacher *trust the path* toward self-insight; the restraint branch prevents the "it invented a problem" cynicism that would poison C. | **Keep, but subordinate.** Diagnosis is the on-ramp, not the destination — it must always visibly *advance the teacher's own thinking*, never showcase Compass's cleverness for its own sake. |
| **Anti-coauthoring exchange** | Not a separate feature demo — it is the **guarantor** of C. If Compass wrote anything, the teacher could not claim the insight as their own. | **Reframe.** Stop treating it as "goal 5 of 6." It exists to protect the authorship of the final insight. Trigger it only if the teacher reaches for the answer. |
| **Instructional sequence** (one step → convention only when needed → scaffold if stuck) | Every step must *converge* on the moment the teacher can state the rule. Meaning-before-convention (D) is required scaffolding: jargon would let Compass supply the concept and short-circuit self-construction. | **Keep, prune ruthlessly.** Cut any turn that demonstrates a feature but does not move the teacher closer to articulating the principle. |
| **Ending / transfer beat** (teacher states the principle; no summary, no praise) | **This IS the aha.** Everything upstream is setup for the teacher's own sentence. | **Elevate to centerpiece.** The whole preview is timed and shaped to reach this one moment cleanly within ~4 minutes. Compass must *resist* stating the principle itself, even when it would be faster. |
| **Exit** ("bring a real assignment and keep going") | Converts C into E: having taught themselves, the teacher extrapolates to their classroom. | **Keep.** The pull is a consequence of C, so the exit should reference *what they just did*, not Compass's features. |

## What this decision changes about the preview
1. **Success is redefined:** the single behavioral proof is *"the teacher produced a self-stated principle in their own words."* If that happens, the preview succeeded even if some secondary goals were lightly touched; if it doesn't, the preview failed even if every feature was shown.
2. **Feature-completeness is explicitly rejected:** we do not try to give all six goals equal airtime. Diagnosis, meaning-before-convention, and anti-coauthoring are demoted to *instruments* of the one aha.
3. **The riskiest failure mode to engineer against** is the arc stalling before the transfer beat. The branch design and time-box must protect the runway to the teacher's own insight above all else.
